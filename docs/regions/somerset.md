# The Somerset Levels

[← The rebuilt landscapes](index)

*The other landscape rebuilt wholesale, and the one where the scoping exercise's terrain
model was silently wrong for a month.*

> **Research leads, not verified citations**, except where marked **verified**.

## Why this region is difficult

The [Levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_Levels){:target="_blank"} are a coastal wetland reclaimed in stages from the Roman period onward, with
**medieval monastic drainage** — Glastonbury, Athelney, Muchelney — followed by piecemeal
enclosure and then by statutory schemes.

Unlike the Fens, a good deal of the medieval work is still *in* the network. Some rhynes
are older than they look, and generic pattern rules that date a channel by its name are
weakest here for exactly that reason: in the Levels and Romney Marsh a *sewer* is a
drainage channel, and many have medieval origins even where the present cut is later.

**And the terrain model here was silently wrong.** The region box marking untrustworthy
terrain had been typed with the wrong easting — 200 km out, landing in open sea off
Cornwall — so every reach on the Levels read as *trustworthy terrain*, on precisely the
pumped and embanked ground the flag exists to mark. Corrected, the Brue reads 0.937
untrusted and King's Sedgemoor Drain 0.487. **Nothing in any national total showed the
error**, which is the standing lesson of this project rather than an anecdote about a typo.

## The schemes, and when

| period | scheme | effect |
|---|---|---|
| Roman | coastal embankment and early reclamation | |
| 12th–15th c. | **monastic drainage** by Glastonbury, Athelney, Muchelney | rhynes and river diversions, **some still in the network** |
| c.1200s | the **River Brue diversion** to Highbridge | a medieval re-routing, not a modern one |
| 17th–18th c. | piecemeal enclosure drainage | |
| **1795** | **[King's Sedgemoor Drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_Sedgemoor_Drain){:target="_blank"}** | a major new channel |
| 19th c. | Huntspill and Brue improvements; pumping | |
| **1940** | **the Huntspill River** | wholly modern |

## Modern scholarship and survey

- **Williams, *The Draining of the Somerset Levels* (1970)** — **verified**, and the
  standard account.
- **Rippon, *The Transformation of Coastal Wetlands* (2000)** — **verified**; the
  comparative study of wetland reclamation, and the best guide to what is medieval here.
- **Rippon on the Brue valley**, and the Somerset Levels Project (Coles) — the wetland
  survey tradition that produced the [Sweet Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Track){:target="_blank"}, and with it a great deal of
  palaeoenvironmental work on where the water was.

## Cartographic and land-record

- **Monastic cartularies and estate records**, for Glastonbury in particular: the drainage
  was carried out by a landowner who kept archives, and the surveys survive.
- **Enclosure awards**, 18th–19th c., which frequently record the older watercourse as a
  boundary.
- **Day and Masters' Somerset (1782)** and the county maps before the OS.

## What to do here first

**Establish which rhynes are medieval.** The generic pattern rules cannot, and are admitted
to be weak. This is a case where a local study answers a question the national method
cannot — and where getting it wrong is invisible: a medieval rhyne dated 1600 is silently
removed from the reconstruction, and nothing in any total will show it.
