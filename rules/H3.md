## Coastal Routing

Having established R-01 for terminating rivers (and streams) at high_water, and with the eventual goal in mind of being able to plan journeys via coastal routes and inland waterways, we need a method for defining routes around the coast - a cost surface. This will be valid at all dates across our temporal window, including 2026, so this, and first R-01, are legitimate Stage 1 infrastructure.

My earlier project https://github.com/docuracy/Historical_Sea_Routing achieved this using https://h3geo.org/, as demonstrated at https://docuracy.github.io/Historical_Sea_Routing/. By limiting the scope to the coastal waters of the British Isles (optimal routes between some English ports can be to the west of Ireland and between Orkney and Shetland), the memory footprint can be contained to manageable levels.

The code was developed without the aid of a coding agent, and you may well find ways to optimise it, but the principles, the algorithms, and the data sources are all sound. The Pages site allows a user to pick two H3 cells, vessel type, seasonality, and various other parameters, and download the GeoJSON mapping the route both ways through a chain of H3 cells. rules/route_871973361ffffff_871874addffffff.geojson illustrates a return journey from Newcastle to Plymouth.

For _this_ project, I think we'll need a grid of H3 cells, banded by decreasing resolution with distance from the coast. Examine the algorithm, which uses a DEM to compute sightlines to high ground - ships' masters preferred to remain within sight of land (the https://github.com/docuracy/Historical_Sea_Routing#references-and-data-sources discuss this, I think - we should link to those in our documentation). The demonstration uses a cell resolution too large to penetrate far into estuaries, so in order to link rivers to the grid we would need bathymetry and an appropriate algorithm to compute paths to cell centres.
